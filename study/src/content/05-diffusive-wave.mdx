# Chapter 5 — Diffusive-Wave Routing

The wide-channel formulas and historical widgets below illustrate the classical law. The current production kernel regularizes very small water-surface slopes and uses a diffusion timestep bound; §5.5 describes these safeguards. The widgets are not production validation tools.

Chapter 4 builds the kinematic wave from the Saint-Venant equations and shows it **translating** a flood pulse downstream without ever changing its shape. That assumption — friction balances gravity, full stop — is exactly right on a steep mountain reach and exactly wrong on a flat valley floor. This chapter is entirely about the gap between those two cases: where the kinematic wave actually gives the wrong answer, what extra physics fixes it, how that physics is written into OPM's production router, and what it costs to run all of it under one single, global time step.

---

## 5.1 — Where Kinematic Wave Fails

Drop a stone in a long, still canal and watch the ripple travel away. It does not stay a sharp spike — it spreads out and gets shorter the farther it goes. A flood wave does the same: the sharp crest that leaves the mountains arrives at the city lower and broader, smeared over a longer time. This flattening is called **peak attenuation**, and the kinematic wave cannot reproduce it — it translates the crest without ever lowering it, so it tends to *over-predict* the peak discharge at a gauge, exactly the number engineers care most about.

Now pour water steadily into one end of a bathtub with the drain open. Near the drain the water is shallow and quick; but if the drain is small, water piles up behind it and the level rises upstream. Rivers do this constantly — a pond, a flat reach, a narrow culvert, or a downstream lake raises the water level and **slows the flow coming in from upstream**. This is the **backwater effect**, and the kinematic wave is blind to it too: it uses only the bed slope, which always points downhill and knows nothing about how deep the water already is in the next cell. A kinematic cell keeps draining at full Manning's rate even when the cell below it is brim-full and physically cannot accept more.

Both pictures correspond to the kinematic-wave number from Chapter 2, $K = S_0\,g\,L / V_0^2$, falling below the safe threshold of about 20:

1. **Flat slopes** ($S_0 < 10^{-3}$) — the bed slope no longer dominates; the water's own depth differences matter as much as gravity.
2. **Peak attenuation** — the kinematic wave keeps the crest sharp; reality flattens it.
3. **Backwater and adverse gradients** — a downstream pool can be *deeper* than the upstream cell, opposing the flow; the kinematic wave never sees it.

## 5.2 — Follow the Water Surface, Not the Bed

The fix is one idea: drive flow with the slope of the **water surface**, not the slope of the **bed**. Define the water-surface elevation as bed plus depth, $\text{WSE} = z + h$, and replace the bed slope $S_0$ with

$$
S_w = S_0 + \frac{h_i - h_\text{ds}}{\Delta x}
$$

Three cases fall out immediately:

- $h_i > h_\text{ds}$ — the upstream cell is fuller than the bed slope alone would suggest. $S_w > S_0$: a small boost.
- $h_i = h_\text{ds}$ — both cells equally full. $S_w = S_0$ exactly: identical to kinematic.
- $h_i < h_\text{ds}$ — the downstream water depth is greater; its water surface need not yet be higher. $S_w < S_0$, and once $h_\text{ds} - h_i$ exceeds $S_0\,\Delta x$, $S_w$ goes **negative**. OPM clamps it: $S_\text{eff} = \max(S_w, 0)$ — flow halts rather than running backward.

Drag $h_i$ and $h_\text{ds}$ below and watch the water-surface line tilt, flatten, and reverse. With $S_0 = 0.001$ and $\Delta x = 100\,\text{m}$, the exact halt threshold is $h_\text{ds} - h_i = 0.10\,\text{m}$ — verify it on the slider.

## 5.3 — Deriving the Diffusive-Wave Equation From Scratch

Chapter 4 showed the full Saint-Venant momentum equation has five terms, and that the kinematic wave keeps only friction and gravity:

$$
\underbrace{\frac{\partial Q}{\partial t}}_{①} +
\underbrace{\frac{\partial (Q^2/A)}{\partial x}}_{②} +
\underbrace{gA\frac{\partial h}{\partial x}}_{③} +
\underbrace{gAS_f}_{④} -
\underbrace{gAS_0}_{⑤} = 0
$$

Kinematic drops ①②③, leaving $S_f = S_0$. The **diffusive wave makes a smaller cut**: it drops only the inertial terms ①② — local and convective acceleration, the two terms responsible for genuine flood-wave dynamics like surging and reflection — but it **keeps** the pressure-gradient term ③. What survives is

$$
S_f = S_0 - \frac{\partial h}{\partial x}
$$

which is exactly the continuous-space version of the discrete $S_w$ from §5.2. Linearizing around a reference depth $h_0$ and velocity $V_0$ (small perturbation $h' = h - h_0$) turns the diffusive-wave momentum balance into an **advection–diffusion equation**:

$$
\frac{\partial h'}{\partial t} + c_k\,\frac{\partial h'}{\partial x} = D\,\frac{\partial^2 h'}{\partial x^2}
$$

$$
c_k = \tfrac{5}{3}\,V_0 \qquad\qquad D = \frac{V_0\,h_0}{2\,S_0}
$$

The celerity $c_k$ is identical to Chapter 4's kinematic celerity — the wave still travels at $\tfrac{5}{3}$ the flow velocity. The new term is $D$, a genuine **diffusivity**: it spreads and lowers the pulse exactly the way heat spreads through a rod. Because $S_0$ sits in the *denominator*, flat slopes ($S_0\to 0$) blow $D$ up — flat reaches diffuse strongly, steep reaches barely diffuse at all:

| Setting | $S_0$ | $D\approx$ (m²/s) | Behavior |
|---|---|---|---|
| Flat valley floor | 0.001 | 0.15 | strong attenuation — diffusive routing matters |
| Moderate slope | 0.01 | 0.015 | mild attenuation |
| Steep hillslope | 0.05 | 0.003 | negligible — kinematic is fine |

## 5.4 — Conveyance Depth and the Current Implementation

$S_w$ alone is not quite enough. On a normal downhill bed the upstream cell's own depth $h_i$ is the right depth to carry the flow. But at a D8 saddle or a local DEM step, the *downstream* cell's ground can sit higher than the upstream cell's ($z_\text{ds} > z_i$) even while its water surface is still lower — water is conveyed across that higher lip, and the relevant depth is the depth of water **over that higher bed**, not over the upstream one.

OPM's `diffusive_wave_discharge()` in `MRRpy/core/routing/hydraulics.py` borrows the answer from the **CASC2D family of models** (Julien et al., 1995; later GSSHA, and the LISFLOOD-FP diffusion wave): use the depth above whichever of the two beds is higher,

$$
h_\text{higher} = \max(\text{WSE}_i, \text{WSE}_\text{ds}) - \max(z_i, z_\text{ds})
$$

A single blend weight $\theta$ ties the slope and the conveyance depth together as a coherent pair: $\theta=0$ reduces to kinematic exactly (own depth, bed slope); $\theta=1$ — OPM's production default — is the full diffusion wave (water-over-higher-bed, water-surface slope). ## 5.5 — Why Level Water Can Control the Explicit Step

The current router recomputes a scheme-specific bound from the wet state. It does not choose the diffusive step solely from the steepest bed slope. Write $Q=K(h)\sqrt{S}$ for positive water-surface slope $S$. Holding conveyance fixed gives

$$
\frac{\partial Q}{\partial(h_i-h_j)}=\frac{K}{2L\sqrt{S}}.
$$

As water levels approach equality, discharge tends to zero but its sensitivity grows without bound. For a wide uniform channel, the linearized diffusivity is $D=h^{5/3}/(2n\sqrt{S})$. Explicit stepping therefore has a diffusion restriction proportional to $\Delta x^2/D$, in addition to its advective restriction. Small velocity is not evidence of a safe large step.

The production kernel uses $\phi_\epsilon(S)=S/\sqrt{\max(S,\epsilon)}$ for nonnegative interior slopes. This keeps discharge zero at level water and bounds its derivative. The controller sums twice the tangent head conductance and conveyance terms over incident edges, including all tributaries, divided by actual storage area. At/below epsilon, doubling the linear conductance prevents the alternating-mode sign reversal found with the earlier positivity-only bound. A narrow receiver can be more restrictive than its donors. `CFL_DT_MIN` is a warning threshold; it cannot override the safe step.

Regularization changes the small-gradient law. Increasing epsilon can speed explicit stepping while changing relaxation. A slope cap or a volume limiter alone does not fix this stiffness. Averaging output can hide oscillation without fixing the state update.

## 5.6 — What an Implicit Solve Can and Cannot Fix

Backward Euler treats the diffusive transfer at the new time level. For linear diffusion it damps stiff modes without the explicit diffusion restriction. Nonlinear routing still requires a converged conservative solve, nonnegative water, boundary handling, and timestep refinement for accuracy. Crank–Nicolson is stable for linear diffusion but can ring after an abrupt release; stability does not imply nonoscillatory behavior.

An acyclic D8 network with one downstream neighbor per cell is a tree or forest. Confluences break a chain's tridiagonal ordering, but **do not require a general sparse solver**. Eliminate leaves toward the roots, then back-substitute: each edge is processed once. This gives an $O(N)$ linear solve per Newton iteration, including nonsymmetric edge Jacobians. Cycles, multiple-flow-direction links, and a full 2-D mesh require a different solver.

The independent prototype in `tools/diffusive_independent_lab.py` checks this tree solve against dense and sparse linear algebra, then tests nonlinear reservoir releases against SciPy Radau. It also tests a flux-variable formulation without slope epsilon. These are research implementations, not production routing options or GPU benchmarks. A contraction makes a large Newton step fail; timestep rejection and retry are essential.

See `docsite/diffusive-flat-water-investigation.md` for measured errors, failures, and source references. The earlier claim that a flux limiter replaces diffusion stability, or that implicit D8 routing is inherently impractical, was incorrect.

## 5.7 — Side-by-Side & Mass Balance

| Condition | $K$ | Recommended | Reason |
|---|---|---|---|
| Steep hillslopes ($S_0 > 0.01$) | $\gg 20$ | Kinematic or diffusive | Nearly identical; diffusive auto-reduces to it |
| Moderate ($S_0 \sim 0.005$–$0.01$) | 20–100 | Either | Small difference |
| Flat valley floors ($S_0 < 0.005$) | $< 20$ | **Diffusive** | Backwater and attenuation matter |
| Urban / ponding areas | varies | **Diffusive** | Stormwater pools |

Diffusive routing can model downstream-stage suppression of flow, but its directed links prohibit reverse flow. It is not a general ponding, floodplain, or dam-break solver. The routing choice must be checked against the event and boundary conditions; bed slope alone is not sufficient.

Adding backwater and flow-halting does not threaten mass conservation. The volume update from Chapter 2 is completely unchanged —

$$
V_i^{n+1} = V_i^n + r_\text{eff}\,\Delta x^2\,\Delta t + Q_\text{in}\,\Delta t - Q_\text{out}^\text{lim}\,\Delta t
$$

— only the *formula* for $Q_\text{out}$ differs between schemes, and both are capped by the identical flux limiter and tracked by the identical mass-balance accumulators. When $S_\text{eff} = 0$, $Q_\text{diff} = 0$: no water leaves the cell, the limiter is trivially satisfied, and the volume is simply unchanged — stopping flow does not lose water.

## 5.8 — Chapter Summary

| Concept | Formula |
|---|---|
| Water-surface elevation | $\text{WSE} = z + h$ |
| Water-surface slope | $S_w = S_0 + (h_i - h_\text{ds})/\Delta x$ |
| Effective slope ($\theta$-blend) | $S_\text{eff} = \max\!\big(S_0 + \theta(h_i-h_\text{ds})/\Delta x,\ 0\big)$ |
| Conveyance depth | $h_\text{higher} = \max(\text{WSE}_i,\text{WSE}_\text{ds}) - \max(z_i,z_\text{ds})$ |
| Flow depth | $h_\text{flow} = (1-\theta)h_i + \theta\,h_\text{higher}$ |
| Discharge | $Q_\text{diff} = \tfrac{1}{n}\,h_\text{flow}^{5/3}\,S_\text{eff}^{1/2}\,\Delta x$ |
| Hydraulic diffusivity | $D = V_0 h_0 / (2 S_0)$ |

**Key takeaways:**

1. The kinematic wave fails on flat slopes, cannot attenuate peaks, and cannot feel backwater — all because it uses the static *bed* slope.
2. The diffusive wave's one idea: drive flow with the **water-surface slope** $S_w$ instead of $S_0$.
3. Three cases — $S_w > S_0$ (normal boost), $S_w = S_0$ (identical to kinematic), $S_w < 0$ (clamped to 0, flow halts).
4. The $\theta$-blend makes $\theta=0$ exactly kinematic and $\theta=1$ fully diffusive; OPM uses $\theta=1$.
5. The CASC2D/GSSHA conveyance depth $h_\text{higher}$ correctly handles adverse slopes and reduces to $h_i$ for normal downhill flow.
6. The `ds_safe` trick replaces the outlet's $-1$ index with 0 and masks the result — a branch-free, GPU-friendly downstream lookup.
7. Nearly level wet cells can determine the global explicit step because head conductance increases as the water-surface gradient vanishes. Positivity alone is insufficient.
8. Implicit tree solves are feasible on acyclic D8 networks. They alleviate explicit stiffness but require nonlinear convergence checks, accuracy controls, and separate GPU validation.
